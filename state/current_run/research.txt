**1. CORE ANSWER**  
An “invisible” (thin‑fabric) zipper snags because its very low‑profile teeth and shallow slider channel provide almost no clearance for the delicate, easily‑deformed fabric that holds the teeth. When a stray fiber, lint, or a tiny wrinkle of the fabric slips into the V‑shaped channel, the slider’s normal force pushes the fabric against the teeth. The fabric‑to‑metal friction coefficient (≈ 0.2) then creates a friction force that can exceed the hand force, locking the slider and pulling the fabric into the teeth. The problem is amplified by close tooth spacing, minimal backing fabric, and the softness of the material. Manufacturers (e.g., YKK’s “Snag‑less” zipper) mitigate it by raising the fabric away from the slider and reshaping the slider’s bridge to keep the cloth out of the channel.

**3. MECHANISM / CAUSE AND EFFECT**  
- **Cause:** Tiny clearance in the slider’s V‑channel combined with a soft, thin fabric tape.  
- **Effect:** A fiber or wrinkle enters the channel → slider’s normal force \(F_n\) increases → friction \(F_f = μF_n\) (μ ≈ 0.2) overtakes hand force → zipper stalls (snag).  
- **Amplifiers:** Low‑profile teeth, single‑layer tape, 2–3 mm tooth pitch (close spacing).  
- **Mitigation:** Raising the fabric away from the channel and reshaping the slider bridge reduces intrusion, lowering \(F_n\) and thus \(F_f\).

**5. MISCONCEPTIONS OR CONTRADICTIONS**  
| Myth | Reality |
|------|----------|
| “Invisible zippers are snag‑proof because the teeth are tiny.” | Tiny teeth reduce clearance, making any fiber more likely to block the channel. |
| “Lubricating always fixes a snag.” | Oil attracts dust/lint; dry lubricants (graphite, silicone wax) are preferred for thin fabrics. |
| “Only fabric causes snags.” | Bent/misaligned teeth or a worn slider bridge can also create an obstruction even with clean fabric. |

---

**6. DISPUTES OR UNCERTAINTY**  
- **Exact friction coefficient (μ)** for specific fabrics (silk, microfiber, high‑finish nylon) is not precisely known; the cited 0.2 value comes from a generic woven belt test.  
- **Dynamic effects** (speed of zipper operation) may temporarily overcome friction, but long‑term snag behavior remains the same.  
- **Long‑term wear**: How oxidation of metal teeth or micro‑abrasion of polymer teeth alters μ over many cycles is still under investigation (YKK internal data not public).
