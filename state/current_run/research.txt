**1. CORE ANSWER**  
Songs get “stuck” in the head because a short, familiar, and easily‑chunked musical fragment is automatically captured by the phonological loop of working memory. Predictive‑coding mechanisms treat the fragment’s ending as a cue for its beginning, creating a self‑reinforcing loop that repeats until attention is redirected, emotional arousal subsides, or the listener consciously suppresses it. The phenomenon—known as an earworm or involuntary musical imagery (INMI)—is normal, highly prevalent, and driven by memory‑prediction processes rather than pathology.

---

**2. STORY BEATS (10‑16 numbered beats)**  

1. **Cue Encounter** – A person hears a catchy snippet (often chorus, lyric‑rich) in the environment.  
2. **Encoding** – The fragment is stored in auditory memory; its brevity (≈ 3‑5 s) fits the phonological‑loop capacity.  
3. **Chunk Formation** – The melody is chunked into 4‑unit groups, matching short‑term memory limits.  
4. **Self‑Cue Loop** – The phrase’s ending mirrors its start, automatically cueing the next replay.  
5. **Loop Initiation** – The fragment begins looping silently in the phonological store.  
6. **Low Cognitive Load** – When the mind is wandering or not engaged in demanding tasks, the loop persists.  
7. **Emotional Modulation** – Higher arousal (fast tempo, stress) can strengthen the loop’s persistence.  
8. **Familiarity Boost** – Well‑known, liked, lyric‑rich sections increase the likelihood of looping.  
9. **Neural Overlap** – Auditory‑imagery regions shared with speech are activated, reinforcing the replay.  
10. **Potential Intrusiveness** – Personality traits (neuroticism, OCD tendencies) may make the loop feel intrusive.  
11. **Displacement** – A competing cognitive task or deliberate suppression can break the loop.  
12. **Decay or Forgetting** – Without reinforcement, the fragment fades from the phonological store.  

---

**3. MECHANISM / CAUSE AND EFFECT**  

- **Phonological‑loop rehearsal** → occupies working‑memory buffer, causing the fragment to repeat (Killingly, Lacherez & Meuter 2021).  
- **Chunking & short‑term memory limits** → 3‑5 s melodies fit the “magical number 4” chunk size, explaining why only brief snippets persist (Snyder 2000; Cowan 2001).  
- **Predictive coding / self‑cued looping** → phrase‑end cues phrase‑start, creating an automatic loop (Snyder 2000; Arthur 2023).  
- **Familiarity, liking, chorus location, lyrics** → increase probability of earworm formation (Liikkanen & Jakubowski 2020).  
- **Mind‑wandering / low load** → free attention allows the loop to run unchecked (diary/lab studies).  
- **Emotional arousal / stress** → heightens loop strength, especially for fast‑tempo fragments (Khayyer et al. 2021).

---

**5. MISCONCEPTIONS OR CONTRADICTIONS**  

| Misconception | Reality (research) |
|---------------|--------------------|
| Only “catchy” pop songs cause earworms. | Earworms also arise from classical pieces, jingles, instrumental tunes (Liikkanen 2012). |
| Earworms are language‑specific. | Non‑linguistic patterns can produce INMI, but music’s tonal structure makes it especially potent. |
| They signal mental‑health problems. | Earworms are universal; only a minority find them intrusive (median ≈ 1.7–3.5 episodes/day). |
| The Zeigarnik effect drives earworms. | Controlled experiments found no truncation advantage (Hyman 2013; McCullough Campbell & Margulis 2015). |
| The brain “gets stuck” because of a defect. | They reflect normal predictive‑memory rehearsal, possibly aiding consolidation (Kubit & Janata 2022). |

---

**6. DISPUTES OR UNCERTAINTY**  

- **Evolutionary purpose** – Whether INMI is a vestige of oral transmission remains speculative (Harvey 2018; Trainor 2018).  
- **Phonological‑loop duration** – How loops exceed the 1‑2 s buffer (chunk‑binding vs. covert rehearsal) is not fully resolved.  
- **Non‑musical sounds** – Can repetitive non‑musical auditory patterns generate true earworms? Preliminary speech‑to‑song illusion work exists, but systematic evidence is lacking.  
- **Neutral vs. intrusive earworms** – Interaction of personality, mood, and stress predicts intrusiveness, but predictive models are still developing.  
- **Self‑cueing verification** – The hypothesis that phrase endings cue starts needs direct experimental testing.
